1. How To Use The Engine

The AI Internship Recommendation Engine is designed to bridge the gap between academic profiling and industry demands. Follow these steps to generate accurate internship matches:

01

Input Variables

Ensure you select a minimum of one technical skill. The system uses a strict threshold; empty skill sets will trigger an execution failure to prevent false positives.

02

Define Constraints

Your CGPA acts as a hard filter. Internships demanding a higher minimum threshold than your profile will be automatically pruned from the final matrix.

03

Execute Analysis

Click "Run Analysis" to fire the independent sub-engines. Results are segmented into Consensus, Content, Hybrid, and Deep Learning matches.

2. Core Algorithms & Architecture

A. TF-IDF Vectors
(Content Engine)

The strictest form of matching. This engine converts both your profile and the internship requirements into numerical vectors using Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF). It then calculates the Cosine Similarity between them. If your skills do not directly align with the text of the required skills, your score here will be low.

B. Collaborative
(Hybrid Engine)

A statistical approach that looks beyond raw skills. It weights your Content Match at 70% and combines it with a 30% Peer Popularity Score derived from historical user interactions. This recommends internships that are highly rated by students with similar profiles.

C. Neural Networks
(Deep Learning)

Powered by PyTorch. We utilize a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with company embeddings. The network analyzes non-linear hidden patterns in the dataset (using ReLU activation functions) to predict your match probability. This score is hybridized (70% Content / 30% Neural Prediction).

3. Match Score Clarifications

  • ↗ Location Overrides: If you select a specific city (e.g., "Ahmedabad"), the engine executes a hard prioritization override. Internships in that city are forced to the top of the feed, even if they have a lower skill match rate.
  • ↗ Zero-Score Skills: If you select "None" for a skill, the engine automatically assigns a mathematical weight of 0 for that slot in the Spider Graph rendering process to prevent data skewing.
  • ↗ Sub-String Matching: In the visualization layer, partial skill matches (e.g., user inputs "Cybersecurity", company requests "Information Security") are awarded a 60% partial relevance score based on domain heuristics.

// Graph Anatomy

The visualizations are rendered dynamically using Chart.js on HTML5 Canvas. The charts employ a radial gradient to maintain the brutalist aesthetic while offering rapid comparative analysis.

Why Radar Charts?
They allow users to instantly spot symmetry. A perfectly filled polygon indicates a universal fit across all top companies, while a skewed shape reveals skill gaps in specific industry sectors.
